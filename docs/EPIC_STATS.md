## 4. Verb–noun co-occurrence (train)

- Observed pairs: 3,568 of 28,033 possible verb×noun combinations (12.7%). The co-occurrence matrix is very sparse, so a pair prior from train counts strongly constrains fusion.
- Nouns per verb: median 20, max 261 (take).
- Verbs per noun: median 10, max 46 (dough).
- Entropy H(noun)=6.61 bits, H(noun|verb)=5.09; H(verb)=4.13, H(verb|noun)=2.61. Knowing the noun removes more uncertainty about the verb than the reverse.
- Validation pairs never seen in train: 238 of 1352 distinct pairs (18%), covering 3.8% of validation segments. A detector restricted to train-seen pairs cannot get these.

## 8. Coverage reference points (label-only variant, validation)

Coverage = mean over videos of |GT pairs ∩ predicted pairs| / |GT pairs|. These are **priors-only references** computed from annotations (no video model), useful to judge how much a model adds. They ignore timing; the tIoU≥0.1 variant would be lower.

| Strategy | K pairs/video | coverage |
|---|---:|---:|
| same K most frequent train pairs for every video | 10 | 14.1% |
| same K most frequent train pairs for every video | 25 | 26.4% |
| same K most frequent train pairs for every video | 50 | 35.7% |
| same K most frequent train pairs for every video | 100 | 46.8% |
| same K most frequent train pairs for every video | 200 | 57.9% |
| oracle nouns-in-video × top-1 train verbs per noun | ~16 | 33.4% |
| oracle nouns-in-video × top-3 train verbs per noun | ~48 | 68.0% |
| oracle nouns-in-video × top-5 train verbs per noun | ~80 | 79.8% |

Reading: the noun-oracle rows show that **recognising which objects appear** in a video, combined with a verb prior per noun, already explains most of the coverage; noun recognition is the main lever for the 40% coverage metric.

## 9. Implications for the coverage strategy (to confirm once the kit arrives)

1. Output many diverse pairs per video. Distinct GT pairs per val video has median 24, so K must be well above that if precision allows (decided by whether actions carry a confidence score in the kit schema).
2. Use a train co-occurrence prior to restrict and rank verb–noun fusions; the matrix is sparse, so the prior removes most impossible pairs.
3. Segments are short (median ~1.6 s) and often overlap; the localisation stride must be ≤1 s, and NMS must allow overlapping different pairs.
4. Long tail: a small number of verbs dominate; use focal loss / balanced sampling and report per-frequency-bucket recall.
5. Video-length mismatch: many EPIC val videos are outside 2–10 min (section 2); check whether the kit clips them.
